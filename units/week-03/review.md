# HPS100: Scientific Revolution — review and guidance

Unofficial student study notes. Consult official course materials for current requirements. Practice questions are not official assessments.

## Revision checklist

- Reconstruct Shapin’s argument using data, claim, warrant, backing, qualifier, and reservation; explain why diversity challenges a uniform story without disproving every possible historical grouping.
- Explain Aristotle’s essences, four causes, and Earth/heavens distinction before explaining the objections to them.
- Distinguish observing an event sequence from seeing a necessary causal connection; distinguish explanation from circular redescription.
- State Bacon’s gradual method and explain why classifying observations already requires background understanding. The sunrise exercise is not simply Hume’s problem of induction.
- Use Vesalius to show that new observation can accompany reverence for ancient authority, and Galileo to show why instrument reliability requires justification.
- Explain why Boyle separated observations from causal speculation and why detailed reports do not eliminate all reliance on testimony.
- Compare Boyle’s effects-to-causes approach, Hobbes’s causes-to-effects approach, and Newton’s crucial-experiment ideal. An alternative explanation need not refute a theory; eliminating one rival need not prove the remaining theory with certainty.
