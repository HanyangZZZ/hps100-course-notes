# HPS100: Induction and deduction — review and guidance

Unofficial student study notes. Consult official course materials for current requirements. Practice questions are not official assessments.

## Revision checklist and traps

- Identify the premises, conclusion, and inference; an indicator word is helpful but not required.
- Test deductive validity by asking whether true premises and a false conclusion are possible together—not whether the conclusion sounds true.
- Explain why affirming the consequent and denying the antecedent permit counterexamples.
- Distinguish inductive support from certainty, and distinguish Hume's deeper justification challenge from ordinary uncertainty.
- Reconstruct all six Toulmin parts. Warrant links data to claim; backing supports warrant. Confidence is not scope.
- Explain both of Hume's routes and why the overall conclusion depends on treating those routes as exhaustive.
- Separate an explanation of why people believe something from a justification for believing it.
- Explanatory inference needs comparison with rivals; adding “probably” is not enough.
- A successful prediction supports but does not deductively prove a theory. A failed prediction only yields the simplified refutation if the relevant conditional and observation hold.
- A third inference type weakens Hume's exhaustive-options premise; it does not automatically solve the problem.

## Instructor guidance for studying and analyzing arguments

- For Argument Analysis 1, select one inferential step in a direct quotation from a Week 2 or Week 3 reading; the quotation must be no longer than one paragraph. A passage describing someone else's argument is acceptable.
- Supply claim, data, warrant, backing, qualifier, and reservation. Infer unstated commitments from context, rather than assuming every part must be explicitly written in the passage.
- Keep entries compact, direct, and straightforward—usually a few sentences, not a page per part. The aim is to see all the connections at once. If no reservation is appropriate, write **none**.
- Apply the spoken correction in the Hume example: justification versus being okay belongs under reservation/scope, not confidence qualifier.
- Memorizing the Latin names of deductive forms is not required. Detailed formal logic proofs are outside this lecture's expected work; PHL245 was recommended for further study.
- Do the take-home exercises yourself to build the skill; the instructor recommends pencil-and-paper practice because that is how the midterm and exam work will be done.
- Study main lecture concepts rather than memorizing names, dates, or every sentence of the readings. The instructor described the multiple-choice component as at least approximately 90% lecture-based, with perhaps a small number of reading-only items; this is guidance, not a supplied exam blueprint.
- Use analysis to narrow disagreement to the actual disputed step while preserving genuine agreement.
- For further philosophy reference, the instructor recommends the peer-reviewed Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy.
